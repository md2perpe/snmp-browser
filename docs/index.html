Browse MIBs. Poll devices. See what changed.

A desktop app for browsing MIB files and polling live values from SNMP-managed devices — switches, routers, and similar network equipment. Point it at your MIB directories, pick a scalar or table, connect over SNMPv1/v2c/v3, and fetch live values in a sortable, diffable table.

Everything you need to poke at a MIB

🌳

MIB parsing

Recursively parses .mib files in one or more directories into a browsable group/table hierarchy, and flags files that fail to parse.

🗂️

Profiles

Group MIB directories into named profiles — e.g. per firmware version — and switch between them instantly.

🧭

Tree & Tables views

Browse the full group hierarchy, or flatten straight to just the SNMP tables when you know what you're after.

🪟

Tabs & split panes

Open multiple tables and scalars in tabs, across up to two side-by-side panes, to compare devices or tables at once.

🔐

SNMPv1 / v2c / v3

Per-tab connection settings, including full v3 security, authentication and privacy configuration.

🔄

Diff mode

Manual or auto-refresh fetching, with a diff mode that highlights rows added, removed, or changed between fetches.

🏷️

Readable formatting

Human-readable column names and DISPLAY-HINT-aware formatting turn raw MIB identifiers and numeric values into something legible.

⏱️

Walk benchmark

Time repeated SNMP walks of any subtree and see min/median/mean/P95/max and standard deviation across the runs.

How it works

1

Point it at your MIBs

Add one or more directories of .mib files to a profile. They're parsed immediately into a group/table hierarchy in the sidebar.

2

Pick a table or scalar

Browse the tree or flatten to tables, then open the node you want in a tab — or right-click it to benchmark a walk instead.

3

Connect and fetch

Fill in the device address and SNMPv1/v2c/v3 credentials for that tab, then fetch once or turn on auto-refresh.

4

Read the results

Sort, resize, and toggle readable names or display-hint formatting. Turn on diff mode to see exactly what changed between fetches.

Frontend

TypeScript + Vite, no framework.

Backend

Tauri 2 (Rust), using snmp2 for SNMP and tree-sitter with a custom ASN.1 grammar to parse MIB files.
